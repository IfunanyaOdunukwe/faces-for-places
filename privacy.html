Privacy policy

Last updated 4 November 2026. Applies to the Faces for Places and Faces for Places Lite watch faces for Garmin watches, and to buying an unlock code for Lite.

In short

  • The face collects nothing about you and I sell no personal data. It has no accounts, ads, analytics or tracking, and there's no server of mine that it talks to.
  • If you buy an unlock code, Stripe handles the payment, and my checkout keeps your watch code and order number so it can show you your code again. See Buying an unlock code.
  • Your location is used on your watch to time the sky and pick your weather.
  • If you choose web weather, a rounded position (about 1 km) goes to the weather service you choose, and nowhere else.

Who I am

Faces for Places is made by Ifunanya Odunukwe, an independent developer in the UK, who is responsible for this policy. Contact: sunshallowed@protonmail.com.

What stays on your watch

  • Location. The face asks for the location permission. It uses your phone's location (as Garmin's weather has it) or your watch's last GPS fix to work out sunrise and sunset, to choose the nearest city ("Nearest to me", full edition) and to know where to get the weather for. This happens on the watch.
  • Activity and health data. The slots you choose (heart rate, steps, Body Battery, stress, sleep score and so on) are read on the watch and only drawn on the screen. The face never sends them anywhere.
  • Settings. Your choices are kept on the watch, and by Garmin when you change them in Garmin's phone app.

What is sent, and to whom

With the default settings (Garmin weather, for where you are), the face sends nothing itself: Garmin's weather reaches the watch through Garmin's own app.

The face fetches weather from the web only when you choose Open-Meteo or OpenWeather as the weather source, or a Custom place as the weather location (fetched from Open-Meteo when the source is Garmin). Then, every 15, 30 or 60 minutes as you choose, the watch asks that service through your phone for the current weather, sending:

  • a latitude and longitude rounded to two decimal places (about 1 km), either your position or the custom place you typed in;
  • for OpenWeather, the API key you entered.

As with any web request, the service also sees the IP address it comes from. Their own policies cover what they do with it: Open-Meteo (which says it deletes its logs after 90 days) and OpenWeather. The answer (temperature, weather, UV index, feels-like temperature, rain chance, humidity, wind and today's high and low) is kept on the watch. You can stop these requests at any time by going back to the Garmin weather source.

Buying an unlock code

This only applies if you buy an unlock code for Lite on the unlock page. The watch itself sends nothing for this: Lite makes a watch code from an identifier the watch gives the app, and shows it in its settings. You type it into the page yourself.

  • Stripe sells the code as the seller of record, through its Onelink service. It takes your payment details, email address, name, billing address and, if it asks, phone number, sends your receipt, and works out tax, under its privacy policy. It also receives your watch code with the order. I can see your order in its dashboard (including your email address and name), but never your card details.
  • My checkout, a small program hosted by Cloudflare, keeps your watch code, order number and whether the order was paid or refunded. It keeps no name or email address. It keeps these so that your code page keeps working, until you ask me to delete them. As with any web request, Cloudflare sees the IP address it comes from.

I use these only to give you your code, to handle refunds and support, and for the records tax law requires.

Garmin

Installing the face, its settings in the phone app and its store page go through Garmin's Connect IQ Store, under Garmin's privacy policy. If the face crashes, Garmin may share a crash report with me (the watch model, its software version and where in the code it failed), which I use only to fix the problem. Store reviews are public on Garmin's store.

If you email me

I use your email address and what you send only to reply and to fix or improve the face, and I don't share them. I delete them when they're no longer needed, and straight away if you ask.

Your rights

Under UK data protection law you can ask what data I hold about you (in practice, emails you've sent me and any unlock code order), and ask me to correct or delete it. Write to the address above. If you're unhappy with my answer, you can complain to the UK Information Commissioner's Office at ico.org.uk.

Children

The face collects no personal data from anyone, children included.

Changes

If what the face sends changes, this page will be updated before the new version is released, with a new date at the top.
